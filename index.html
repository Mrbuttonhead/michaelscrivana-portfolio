The Single Standard
Brand governance · guidelines · agency enablement
The situation

One A Day relaunched its identity across 50+ SKUs and more than a thousand creative assets. Multiple agencies, every retail and digital channel, one relaunch window. The brand book existed. It was a PDF, and it lived wherever the last person had emailed it.

What I did

I digitized the brand book into the single standard every agency and internal team accessed and worked from — one live resource instead of a circulating file — then coordinated pack renders across every SKU and held consistency through Amazon (including A+ pages), Walmart, Target, retail, digital and social. I wrote the design briefs and reviewed partner deliverables against the standard rather than against taste.

Also on the record

I authored the 15-page ecommerce content standards brief defining best-in-class specs for Amazon, Walmart and Target, and co-authored Bayer Consumer Health's 189-slide Inclusive Design standard — WCAG AA contrast adopted across print, web, mobile, pack and POS, written for internal colleagues and external agency partners.

Asset Taxonomy & the Digital Shelf
Naming · tagging · publishing · content governance
Publishing at volume

I oversaw 700+ product renders through GS1 syndication into EDAM/PIM — the naming, tagging, versioning and publishing discipline that decides whether the right image appears on the right retailer's page. Set the aesthetic standards with the render vendors; owned the pipeline that carried them out.

The schema underneath

I designed the PX Taxonomy Framework — five facets across six craft sub-homes, on a canonical-object schema with cross-references — so a person or a machine can find the one authoritative version of a thing rather than the nearest copy of it.

Components, not files

I built master-and-adaptation template systems, modular assets held in a DAM, and reusable regulated-copy modules, so brands, markets and partners assemble from parts rather than commissioning finished files. I design the global masters local markets adapt, and I build and maintain the Figma library and token base for the PX design system.

Self-Service That Displaces the Agency Brief
Scalable tools · adoption · reducing external reliance
The premise

We default to briefing outward because it is the path of least resistance, not because the work needs an agency. Change the path and the default changes with it. Five internal tools in twelve months, all built to be used by people who are not designers.

<1 min
Artwork review against regulatory and brand-system rules — down from 15–20 minutes, and it catches the single-character marks people miss.
60 sec
One SKU through five channels with three regulated tweaks, for Amazon Prime Day.
6 / 6
Craft disciplines in active use of the self-service hub, from zero, inside twelve months.
days not weeks
Internal delivery speed against a typical 4–6-week agency cycle.
How the rules got inside the tools

The compliance layer runs on what I call a brand brain — brand guidelines encoded as machine-readable rules, plus reference assets and scoring, consulted at every step of the pipeline. Output is on-guideline by construction rather than by review. The same architectural move as the digitized brand book, one level down.

Where it ships

The hub sits behind corporate single sign-on so it is a work tool, not a curiosity. I ship agents callable from the tools people already live in, because adoption is mostly the removal of a trip — not evangelism.

Enablement That Outlives the Teacher
Training programs · self-service resources · stakeholder education
The program

I co-authored Bayer Consumer Health's org-wide Design Capability Curriculum — five modules covering cognitive science applied to branding, writing remarkable briefs, the 4D design process, giving feedback, and a brand case study. It launched as required training inside the marketing organization. I delivered three sessions personally to 100+ marketers, then it was published to the internal learning platform as mandatory training that still runs without me, and later extended into the Science organization.

The failure that taught me the most

Briefs got better. They also kept stalling. When I went looking for why, the cause was not missing knowledge — people knew what a good brief contained. It was missing information, and the cost of going to find it. So I stopped teaching the module harder and built the Brief Translator to close the gap instead.

The pattern, stated plainly

Every time I have taught something, I have followed the teaching with a change to the situation rather than to the person. Drawing students were told to measure rather than trust their eye. The accessibility standard is not just 189 slides — it is a contrast check sitting in the QC gate. Brand standards became machine-readable rules the pipeline consults. A written standard is necessary and never sufficient; what makes it survive a deadline is a check that runs at the moment of the decision.
