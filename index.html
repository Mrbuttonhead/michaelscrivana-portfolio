The Single Standard
Brand governance · guidelines · agency enablement
The situation

One A Day relaunched its identity across 50+ SKUs and more than a thousand creative assets. Multiple agencies, every retail and digital channel, one relaunch window. The brand book existed. It was a PDF, and it lived wherever the last person had emailed it.

What I did

I turned the PDF into a live brand website — the single standard every agency and internal team worked from. Not a document to download, but a hub: Brand Strategy, Visual Identity, Brand World Design, a Download Center, FAQ and Contact, with the logo system (Glow, Flat and Pack), the “1” outline, branding bar, color and typography, photography, icons and key-visual rules all in one place. The Download Center served master assets by collection and life stage, so partners pulled the authoritative file instead of the nearest copy. Then I coordinated pack renders across every SKU and held consistency through Amazon (including A+ pages), Walmart, Target, retail, digital and social. I wrote the design briefs and reviewed partner deliverables against the standard.

Also on the record

I authored the 15-page ecommerce content standards brief defining best-in-class specs for Amazon, Walmart and Target, and co-authored Bayer Consumer Health's 189-slide Inclusive Design standard — WCAG AA contrast adopted across print, web, mobile, pack and POS, written for internal colleagues and external agency partners.

Asset Taxonomy & the Digital Shelf
Naming · tagging · publishing · content governance
Publishing at volume

I oversaw 700+ product renders through GS1 syndication into EDAM/PIM. Naming, tagging, versioning and publishing is what decides whether the right image reaches the right retailer's page. I set the standards with the render vendors and owned the pipeline that carried them out.

The schema underneath

I designed the Product Experience (PX) Taxonomy Framework: six facets across six craft sub-homes, on a canonical-object schema with cross-references. A person or a machine can find the authoritative version of a thing, not the nearest copy.

Built from parts

I built master-and-adaptation template systems, modular assets in a DAM, and reusable regulated-copy modules, so brands, markets and partners assemble what they need instead of commissioning it. I design the global masters that local markets adapt, and I maintain the Figma library and token base for the PX design system.

Self-Service That Displaces the Agency Brief
Scalable tools · adoption · reducing external reliance
The premise

Teams brief outward because it is the easiest route, not because the work needs an agency. Change the route and the default changes with it. Five internal tools in twelve months, all built for people who are not designers.

<1 min
Artwork review against regulatory and brand-system rules — down from 15–20 minutes, and it catches the single-character marks people miss.
60 sec
One SKU through five channels with three regulated tweaks, for Amazon Prime Day.
6 / 6
Craft disciplines in active use of the self-service hub, from zero, inside twelve months.
days not weeks
Internal delivery speed against a typical 4–6-week agency cycle.
How the rules got inside the tools

The compliance layer runs on what I call a brand brain: guidelines encoded as machine-readable rules, plus reference assets and scoring, consulted at every step of the pipeline. Work comes out on-guideline by construction, so review catches less. Same move as the digitized brand book, one level down.

Where it ships

The hub sits behind corporate single sign-on, which is what makes it a work tool. I ship agents callable from the software people already have open, because adoption is mostly the removal of a trip.

One of the five, in detail — CreativeLab

The clearest of the five is CreativeLab, a node-based creative production pipeline I built solo, full-stack. It is a React Flow graph editor over image-generation backends: each step — concept → variant → compliance-check — is a node, and the graph itself becomes the artifact, so the next campaign starts from a pipeline rather than a blank brief. The brand brain is wired in as a first-class input, so output is on-guideline by construction rather than corrected back onto brand after the fact.

The node output, animated

The end of that graph, run out: Women’s 50+ Multi Gummy — pack reference in, the hero still iterated, then the keeper frame animated by the video node. Nothing photographed; the whole chain runs inside the tool in minutes.

Enablement That Outlives the Teacher
Training programs · self-service resources · stakeholder education
The program

I co-authored Bayer Consumer Health's org-wide Design Capability Curriculum — five modules covering cognitive science applied to branding, writing remarkable briefs, the 4D design process, giving feedback, and a brand case study. It launched as required training inside the marketing organization. I delivered three sessions personally to 100+ marketers, then it was published to the internal learning platform as mandatory training that still runs without me, and later extended into the Science organization.

The failure that taught me the most

Briefs got better. They also kept stalling. When I went looking for why, it turned out people knew perfectly well what a good brief contained. What they lacked was the information itself, and the time to go find it. So I stopped teaching the module harder and built the Brief Translator.

It reads an incoming request against the brief standard and figures out which inputs are actually missing — the strategy, claims, regulatory or asset details a project cannot move without. Then it points to where each one lives and which team owns it, routing the ask to the right function instead of leaving the requester to guess. The result is a project folder that starts complete, so the work can continue rather than stall waiting on information nobody knew to chase.

The pattern

Every time I have taught something, I have followed it by changing the situation instead of the person. The 189-slide accessibility standard put a contrast check in the QC gate. Brand standards became machine-readable rules. A written standard rarely survives a deadline. A check that runs at the moment of the decision does.

An Announcement That Had to Hold Across Every Channel at Once
External communications · corporate channels · regulated claims
The situation

Bayer replaced the PVC in the Aleve Feminax blister with PET, making it recycle-ready, and positioned it in its own newsroom as a first-of-its-kind in the consumer health sector. A corporate announcement lands everywhere on the same day: press, employees, social, print, the corporate channel. Every surface has to carry the same claim in the same words.

What I made

I built the film end to end: all of the 3D creative, lighting, rendering, animation and typography. The clear PET blister lit against blue, a silver-to-clear wipe for the material change, and an edge-on shot where you can see the weight come out of the pack.

Thirty-two seconds. Published 1 November 2024 on Bayer’s global corporate channel.

Where it ran

The film went out on Bayer’s global corporate channel, not a brand channel. The campaign carried across press and media assets, LinkedIn, the BayerNet employee intranet and print, timed to the newsroom release, and the packaging and healthcare trade press picked it up.

Independently verifiable: the film sits on Bayer’s own corporate channel, and the launch is on the bayer.com newsroom, dated 30 October 2024.

The part that makes it governance

Every statistic here is a regulated environmental claim. Carbon footprint down 38%, 78% less water, 53% less land, packaging weight down 18%, each carrying its comparator: per unit, against the current Aleve blister packaging. Drop the footnote and the claim is no longer substantiated, so it has to survive every crop, every aspect ratio and every channel the asset gets rebuilt for. I worked through that with the communications and sustainability teams, on creative I had built myself.

Same move, different register

The brand book, the machine-readable rules and this asterisk are the same problem at three scales. A claim is only as good as its weakest reproduction.
